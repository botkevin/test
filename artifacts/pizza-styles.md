# Pizza Styles: A Practical Guide

Purpose
- Provide a concise, practical reference for common pizza styles, with dough parameters, baking approach, and signature traits.
- Audience: home bakers and product teams documenting culinary domains; assumes basic dough and oven familiarity.

Classification quick map
- Crust: ultra-thin (Roman tonda, St. Louis), thin (NY, New Haven, Tavern), medium (Neapolitan), thick/pan (Grandma, Sicilian), deep/pie (Detroit, Chicago deep-dish)
- Bake: stone/steel deck (Neapolitan/NY/New Haven/Roman), pan-oil (Grandma/Sicilian/Detroit/Chicago), coal/wood specialty (Neapolitan/New Haven)
- Shape: round (most), rectangle/square (Roman al taglio, Sicilian, Detroit, Grandma)

Key terms
- Hydration: water as % of flour weight. Higher = airier, more open crumb; lower = crisper.
- Preferment: biga/poolish/sponge to boost flavor and structure.
- Thickness factor: dough weight per area (g/in²) guiding crust thickness.

Style profiles

1) Neapolitan (VPN-style)
- Dough: 58–65% hydration; 00 flour; 2–3% salt; minimal yeast; often room-temp bulk/ball 8–24h or cool 24–48h.
- Form: 10–12 in (250–300 mm), soft cornicione, leoparding.
- Bake: 430–480°C (800–900°F) wood-fired deck, 60–90 sec.
- Sauce/Cheese: simple crushed tomatoes; fresh mozzarella fior di latte or bufala; fresh basil; extra virgin olive oil.
- Notes: tender, moist center; minimal toppings.

2) New York slice
- Dough: 60–65% hydration; high-gluten flour; 2–3% oil optional; 24–72h cold ferment.
- Form: 16–18 in pie, foldable slices.
- Bake: 290–330°C (550–625°F) deck; home: steel at max temp; 5–8 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: seasoned tomato sauce; low-moisture mozzarella; light sprinkle of hard cheese optional.
- Notes: balance of chew and crisp; reheat friendly.

3) New Haven “apizza”
- Dough: 60–65% hydration; long ferment; slightly thinner than NY.
- Bake: coal or hot deck ~315–370°C (600–700°F); pronounced char; 7–10 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: tomato-forward; white clam pie is iconic (no red sauce).
- Notes: crisp, charred, irregular rim.

4) Roman tonda (scrocchiarella)
- Dough: ~55–60% hydration; low yeast; 24–48h ferment.
- Form: very thin, crackly round.
- Bake: ~330–350°C (625–660°F) deck; 2–3 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: restrained; high-quality toppings.
- Notes: shatter-crisp base.

5) Roman al taglio (by the cut)
- Dough: 75–85% hydration; often biga/poolish; long cold ferment.
- Form: rectangular sheets; sold by weight.
- Bake: electric/deck 250–300°C (480–570°F), often par-baked then topped; 8–15 min total.
- Sauce/Cheese: highly varied; post-bake garnishes common.
- Notes: airy, open crumb; olive-oil brushed pan.

6) Sicilian (sfincione)
- Dough: 65–75% hydration; sponge/biga common.
- Form: thick rectangular pan, well-oiled.
- Bake: 230–260°C (450–500°F), 12–20 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: often cheese under sauce; breadcrumbs and anchovy traditional in Palermo variants.
- Notes: plush crumb with fried-edge crunch.

7) Detroit style
- Dough: 70–75% hydration.
- Form: 10×14 in blue-steel pan; edge-to-edge cheese.
- Bake: 260–290°C (500–550°F), 12–15 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: Wisconsin brick cheese (or blend); sauce stripes added last (“red top”).
- Notes: caramelized cheese frico on edges.

8) Chicago deep-dish
- Dough: lower hydration (~45–55%); enriched with oil (often corn/vegetable); some use a bit of semolina.
- Form: high-sided round pan; layered assembly.
- Bake: 205–225°C (400–435°F), 25–35 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: sliced low-moisture mozzarella layered first, toppings, then crushed-tomato sauce on top.
- Notes: buttery, flaky bite; fork-and-knife.

9) Chicago tavern-cut thin
- Dough: moderate-low hydration (~50–58%); sometimes baking powder; docked.
- Form: very thin, crispy; cut into squares.
- Bake: 260–290°C (500–550°F), 10–15 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: zesty sauce; mozzarella/provolone blends.
- Notes: bar snack heritage; crackery texture.

10) St. Louis style
- Dough: unleavened or minimal yeast; cracker-crisp with baking powder; oil enriched.
- Form: ultra-thin; square-cut.
- Bake: ~230–260°C (450–500°F), 8–12 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: sweet-leaning sauce; Provel processed cheese blend is signature.
- Notes: very crisp, low moisture.

11) Grandma pizza
- Dough: NY-style dough; 60–65% hydration; long cold ferment.
- Form: shallow rectangular pan, olive-oil coated; thinner than Sicilian.
- Bake: 250–290°C (480–550°F), 10–15 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: often cheese first then dollops/stripes of sauce; garlic, basil.
- Notes: crisp bottom, light interior.

12) California style
- Dough: similar to thin NY/Neo hybrids; preferments common.
- Form: thin round; artisanal.
- Bake: hot deck/stone; 4–8 min.
- Sauce/Cheese: creative, produce-driven toppings (e.g., barbecue chicken, arugula, goat cheese).
- Notes: emphasis on seasonal ingredients.

Home-oven success tips
- Use a steel or stone; preheat 45–60 min at max temp. For blistering tops, finish 60–120 sec under broiler (watch closely).
- For pan styles, coat pan with 1–2 tbsp olive oil; proof dough in pan; avoid degassing edges.
- Cold ferment 24–72h for flavor; rest dough at room temp 60–120 min before stretching.
- Target dough weights (approx.):
  - 12 in Neapolitan/NY: 240–280 g
  - 14 in NY: 350–400 g
  - 16 in NY: 450–550 g
  - 10×14 in Detroit: 550–650 g
  - 18×13 in Grandma/Sicilian: 700–900 g

Glossary
- Biga: stiff preferment (typically 45–60% hydration).
- Poolish: equal parts flour/water (100% hydration) with a pinch of yeast.
- Frico: caramelized cheese crust formed against the pan walls.
- Thickness factor: dough grams per square inch (e.g., NY ~0.08–0.1; Grandma ~0.1–0.12; Sicilian/Detroit ~0.12–0.14+).

At-a-glance reference (typical ranges)
- Neapolitan: 58–65% hydration, 430–480°C, 1–1.5 min
- NY: 60–65%, 290–330°C, 5–8 min
- New Haven: 60–65%, 315–370°C, 7–10 min
- Roman tonda: 55–60%, 330–350°C, 2–3 min
- Roman al taglio: 75–85%, 250–300°C, 8–15 min
- Grandma: 60–65%, 250–290°C, 10–15 min
- Sicilian: 65–75%, 230–260°C, 12–20 min
- Detroit: 70–75%, 260–290°C, 12–15 min
- Chicago deep-dish: 45–55%, 205–225°C, 25–35 min
- Tavern thin: 50–58%, 260–290°C, 10–15 min
- St. Louis: unleavened/minimal yeast, 230–260°C, 8–12 min

Notes and variability
- Parameters vary by flour type, yeast activity, and oven. Treat ranges as starting points; adjust for your equipment and taste.
- Regional names often overlap; pizzerias blend techniques and toppings.
